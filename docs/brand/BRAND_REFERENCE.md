## Identity and visual evidence

- Customer-facing name: **Snacks Republic by PJRB**, as shown on the main logo. Preserve the plural “Snacks”; some secondary artwork uses a shortened name.
- Main logo: red circular plate/house motif with crossed spoon and fork; bold golden-yellow lettering, white outline, red byline.
- Primary look: pink-and-cream gingham, deep-red headings, golden-yellow highlights, greenery, large food/drink images.
- Secondary look: warm cream, pale peach checks, burgundy lettering, kraft/wood framing on the noodles and street-food menus.
- Tone: cheerful, casual, food-focused, and welcoming. The source supports a snack-and-drink shop covering meals as well as beverages.

## Screen direction for brainstorming

**Storefront:** cream canvas, red/yellow brand marks, subtle pink gingham behind the opening hero, and clear “Explore the menu” and “Start an order” actions. Lead with colorful fruity sodas, citrus, ice, and bubbles; feature the food menu in supporting sections. Suggested hero headline: “Fruity sips. Happy bites.” Feature sandwiches and noodles without labeling them bestsellers unless sales data supports that claim.

**Menu:** make Fruity Soda the primary beverage category, alongside Silog Meals, Kanto Foods, Samyang Noodles, and Sandwiches. The separate Strawberry Summer Drink remains a draft under other drinks until its details are configured. Show portion and price together. Restrict choices to those supported by each product. Food portions and add-ons use their own controls.

**POS:** cream/white work surfaces, red selected state, compact brand header, quick search, category tabs, food thumbnails, persistent desktop cart, and a mobile cart sheet. Keep patterned graphics outside busy checkout controls. Show piece counts, serving size, and selected add-ons clearly on the preparation ticket and receipt.

**Mobile:** stacked hero, naturally scrolling categories, responsive product cards, large touch targets, full-width customization sheet, and a sticky cart summary above the phone safe area. Keep all main information in live HTML text; poster images cannot serve as the only menu.

**Motion:** short section entrances and gentle product emphasis; reduced-motion alternative; no scroll hijacking or auto-advancing menu content. Decorative leaves/checks must not overlap controls.

**Asset handling:** retain these originals as reference material. Several screenshots contain corner UI overlays and baked-in promotional text. Do not stretch whole posters into product thumbnails, copy overlays into the site, or redraw/distort the logo. Request clean logo/product assets when preparing final production imagery. Any future raster cleanup uses the image-editing workflow; no image edits were performed in this review.

## Database implications

- Keep shared catalog/order/inventory services across all categories.
- Use sellable product variants for both drink sizes and food portions. Replace the drink-only sizes concept with serving_options (12 oz, 16 oz, 22 oz, 1 stick, 3 pieces, 4 pieces, and a default serving where appropriate).
- A sellable variant has a non-null serving_option_id; enforce a unique product/serving pair for the initial single-serving-dimension design. Flavor choices belong to product-scoped option groups and affect recipes when needed.
- Store serving labels in order snapshots. A quantity of 2 for a 3-piece siomai variant means two servings/six pieces; recipe quantities are per serving.
- Store noodle add-on prices once per allowed variant-option relationship; order-item options snapshot names, prices, and quantities.
- Missing prices must be nullable on unpublished draft variants; publishing/ordering requires a configured nonnegative price. Zero is an explicit free price, never a missing-value fallback.
- Ingredient/packaging recipes must come from actual operations, never estimates derived from photographs. Keep recipe-dependent live inventory checkout disabled until its recipes are configured.
